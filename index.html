Intelligent Multi‑Agent Mobile Manipulation

Teams of mobile robots that jointly manipulate shared objects through contact, force exchange and coupled dynamics, on the ground, in the air, underwater and in space.

Call for contributions See the program

Scope

When robots share an object, their dynamics stop being separate.

Multi-agent mobile manipulation is about teams of mobile robots that jointly manipulate shared objects or interact physically with their surroundings, through contact, forces and coupled dynamics. The same central problem appears whether the team flies, drives, swims, floats in orbit or reassembles itself.

Recent advances in distributed autonomy, perception and robot learning make more capable collective action possible, yet coordination under physical coupling remains a fundamental barrier. We bring ground, aerial, underwater, space and modular robotics together on purpose: comparing embodiments is how we separate domain-specific limitations from fundamental, transferable problems.

In scope

  • Several mobile robots cooperating on one object or one physical interaction
  • Contact, force exchange and coupled dynamics between robots, object and environment
  • Ground, aerial, underwater, space, modular and heterogeneous teams

Out of scope

  • Purely navigational coordination
  • Task allocation without joint physical interaction
  • Standalone, single-robot manipulation
  • Ground
  • Aerial
  • Underwater
  • Space
  • Modular
  • Heterogeneous teams

Three questions

The whole day is organized around three open questions.

Every talk, poster and discussion slot feeds one of them, and they are the backbone of the roadmap we will write with participants.

01

Physical coordination

How can teams plan and regulate shared contacts, wrenches and motions under coupled dynamics and actuation limits?

02

Distributed interaction perception

How can robots jointly infer object state, contact and forces under uncertainty and partial observations?

03

Scalability and validation

Which safety, recovery and performance principles transfer across embodiments, and how should they be benchmarked?

Call for contributions

Bring your work to the poster sessions.

We will accept up to 20 contributions. Each one gets a short spotlight in the lightning-talk session and a poster in one of the two one-hour poster sessions, where invited speakers are encouraged to join the discussion.

Topics

  • Cooperative manipulation and transport with contact and force exchange
  • Planning and control of shared contacts, wrenches and motions under coupled dynamics and actuation limits
  • Joint estimation of object state, contact and forces from partial, distributed observations
  • Learning for collective physical interaction
  • Safety, recovery and failure handling in physically coupled teams
  • Benchmarks, challenge tasks and validation across ground, aerial, underwater, space and modular robots

Early-stage work, negative results and position papers are welcome. Contributions are selected for relevance to joint physical manipulation and for their potential to start a discussion. Contributions are non-archival.

Format, page limit and submission portal will be announced with the call.

Community roadmap

The outcome is a shared research roadmap.

The workshop does not end at 17:40. Participants help write a public roadmap of common bottlenecks, transferable principles, challenge tasks and benchmarks, and the final version will live on this page.

  1. Before

    Questions out, prompts in

    We circulate the three guiding questions and collect discussion prompts from participants.

  2. During

    Panel, breakouts, synthesis

    The panel compares cross-domain bottlenecks. Breakout groups record shared bottlenecks, embodiment-specific constraints and benchmarkable challenge tasks in a common template; rapporteurs summarize them for the whole room.

  3. After

    Draft, comment, publish

    We draft the roadmap, invite participants to comment, and post the revised version here, together with slides, recordings and posters where speakers allow.
